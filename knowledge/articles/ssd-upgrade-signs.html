Performance guide

Signs your computer may benefit from an SSD upgrade

Slow startup can have several causes. Storage is one factor to investigate before deciding whether an upgrade makes sense.

Look for repeated storage delays

If a computer takes a long time to start, open files or load applications, storage performance may be one possible factor. Other causes include limited memory, background applications, software issues, aging hardware or a device fault. Observe when the slowdown occurs rather than assuming a single part is responsible.

Check the device and storage first

Before replacing a drive, identify the computer model and current storage type, check available capacity and consider whether there are signs of device failure. Back up important files before making changes. If a drive may be failing, seek advice before running repeated diagnostics or attempting repairs that could increase risk to data.

Confirm upgrade compatibility

Different computers support different drive formats and capacities. A technician can help confirm compatibility and discuss whether the device can be upgraded. The upgrade may also require operating-system or data-transfer planning.

Consider the whole system

An SSD upgrade cannot fix every performance issue. If the computer has broader faults, repair or other upgrades may be more appropriate. Avoid making a purchase based only on a generic symptom.

Ask about SSD and RAM upgrades or request computer troubleshooting.
