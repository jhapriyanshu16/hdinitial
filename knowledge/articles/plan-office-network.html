Office infrastructure guide

How to plan an office networking setup

A clear view of users, devices and workspaces helps shape a useful conversation about office connectivity.

Map people and devices

List the teams, computers, printers, servers and other systems that need to connect. Note which devices are fixed, which are portable and where people work. This provides a starting point for understanding capacity and coverage without prescribing hardware too early.

Understand the physical layout

Describe the rooms, floors, walls, equipment areas and existing cabling or network hardware. A site layout can help identify practical installation questions and where additional assessment may be useful.

Include security and operations

Consider which users need access to which systems, how guest devices are handled and who will maintain the network. If the office uses servers, CCTV or attendance systems, include them in the discussion because they may share infrastructure.

Plan for change

Think about expected growth, new locations or future equipment. A design should make sense for current needs while allowing the organisation to discuss likely changes.

Never share passwords or network credentials in an initial enquiry. Detailed configuration should be handled through appropriate secure processes.
